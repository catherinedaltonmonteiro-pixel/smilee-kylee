Meet Kylee

Before Kylee Monteiro became a name people saw in the news, she was our sister, our niece, our granddaughter, our friend— and the girl who could walk into a room and somehow make everyone smile.

Her Story

Before “Smilee Kylee”

Kylee was born on November 4, 2006. Her father lovingly called her “Fat Baby” when she was little, a nickname she thought was hilarious and continued laughing about for years.

She lost her father when she was still a baby, in June 2008. Kylee was raised primarily by her mother, a woman who loved her fiercely and gave her everything she could, even when they had very little.

They experienced poverty together, but Kylee never doubted that her mother loved her.

Then, when Kylee was only 13, she lost her mother three days before Christmas. It broke her heart.

Growing Up

And Somehow, She Thrived.

Her aunt and uncle opened their home to Kylee and her sister, Hailey, welcoming them with open arms.

Kylee suddenly had to navigate a new home, new schools, new friendships, and a life she never expected to have.

The quiet, shy little girl began to blossom into someone completely different.

Kylee became outgoing, social, funny, and incredibly easy to love. She made friends everywhere she went.

She loved makeup, music, helping people, talking to her friends, and—perhaps most of all—making people laugh.

And that laugh.

Kylee had one of those ridiculous, contagious laughs that could fill a room. Sometimes you didn't even know what she was laughing about, but you would find yourself laughing right alongside her.

The Name That Stuck

Becoming Smilee Kylee

Kylee had a way of bringing light with her. That light became part of her name.

During the summer before her senior year, Kylee participated in a welding program. The people around her began calling her “Smilee Kylee” because of the way she lit up every room.

The name stuck. So did the smile.

Kylee discovered a real passion for welding. She graduated from Attleboro High School in 2025 and was incredibly proud of what she had accomplished.

She even became Prom Queen.

She had dreams, plans, a future. She was just beginning to build.

A New Future

The Baby Who Changed Everything

At a time when Kylee was struggling deeply with her mental health, she went to Butler Hospital after experiencing suicidal thoughts.

While she was there, she found out she was pregnant.

And something changed.

For the first time, Kylee felt like she had something—and someone—to live for. It wasn't just her anymore.

Kylee fell completely in love with her baby. She sent her family pictures of the hospital paperwork showing the positive result. She sent pictures of baby clothes and talked about the baby constantly.

“If it’s a girl, I want to name her Eleanor and call her Ella.”

She even created a social-media post with her beloved Elmo blanket shaped into a heart, with the words “Baby Groom” in the middle.

That was how much this baby meant to her. She was already a mother in her heart.

Kylee was willing to put her dream of becoming a welder on hold because she didn't want to do anything that could put her baby at risk.

She was already imagining her future as a mother.

She should have gotten to live it.

She should have gotten to hold her baby for the first time, surrounded by the people who loved her. We imagine Kylee smiling through happy tears while her family filled the hospital room, everyone waiting for their chance to hold the precious baby she had created.

She should have gotten to become a mother. She should have gotten to build her welding career. She should have gotten married, grown older, made mistakes, made memories, laughed until she cried, and continued making everyone around her smile.

She should have had a lifetime.

Instead, Kylee was taken from us at only 18 years old. And her baby was taken with her.

The Things We Remember

The Little Things That Were Kylee

Kylee had two things she couldn't sleep without: Bob, her beloved Minion stuffed animal, and her Elmo blanket.

That blanket had been used by her own parents to wrap her in when she was a baby. Kylee couldn't wait to wrap her own baby in it someday.

When she was 16, she once made her sister Faith drive all the way home to get Bob and her Elmo blanket because she couldn't sleep without them.

Bob Her beloved Minion stuffed animal and one of the comforts she carried with her from childhood.
Her Elmo Blanket The blanket her parents wrapped her in as a baby—and the blanket she dreamed of someday wrapping around her own child.
Pieces of Home They weren't just objects. They were pieces of her childhood, her mom and dad, and the feeling of home.

Neither Bob nor the Elmo blanket was ever recovered after Kylee's death. Sometimes, it's the smallest things that hurt the most.

Because we don't just miss Kylee. We miss everything she was supposed to become.

Remember Her This Way

Kylee Was More Than How Her Life Ended

Kylee was more than a missing-person poster. More than a news story. More than a tragedy.

She was the little girl who laughed at being called “Fat Baby.”

She was the daughter who wanted desperately to make her mother proud.

She was the sister who could make an ordinary night unforgettable.

She was the girl who loved makeup, music, welding, her friends, her cat Bleb, Bob, and her Elmo blanket.

She was the Prom Queen.

She was Smilee Kylee.

And she was a young woman who had finally begun to believe in her future.

Kylee had an incredible ability to find light in dark places. Even after losing so much, she kept finding reasons to smile.

Her Legacy

Her Memory Becomes Someone Else's Hope.

Now, we want to take that light and use it to help other people find their way out of the dark.

Because Kylee's story isn't only about what was taken from her. It's about what she still has the power to give.

Hope. Love. Purpose. A reason to keep going.

We can't give Kylee back the lifetime she deserved. But we can make sure her life changes someone else's.

We can help women who are struggling with their mental health. We can support women who are pregnant and scared. We can help women who need safety, stability, and someone who won't give up on them.

We can become the people Kylee needed.

That is her legacy.
That is Smilee Kylee.
