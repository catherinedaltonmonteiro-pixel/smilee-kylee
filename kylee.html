November 4, 2006 — August 19, 2025

Meet Kylee

Before Kylee Monteiro became a name people saw in the news, she was our sister, our niece, our granddaughter, our friend— and the girl who could walk into a room and somehow make everyone smile.

Her Story

Before “Smilee Kylee”

Kylee was born on November 4, 2006. Her father lovingly called her “Fat Baby” when she was little—a nickname she thought was hilarious and continued laughing about for years.

She lost her father in June 2008, while she was still a baby. Kylee was raised primarily by her mother, a woman who loved her fiercely and gave her everything she could, even during times when they had very little.

They experienced poverty, but Kylee never had to question whether her mother loved her.

Then, when Kylee was only 13, she lost her mother just three days before Christmas.

It was another enormous loss for a girl who had already experienced more grief than most children should ever have to carry.

Growing Up

And Somehow, She Thrived.

Her aunt and uncle opened their home to Kylee and her sister, Hailey, welcoming them into their family.

Suddenly, Kylee had to adjust to a different home, different schools, new friendships, and a life she never expected to be living.

And somehow, through everything she had already endured, Kylee began to blossom.

The quiet, shy little girl became outgoing, social, funny, and incredibly easy to love. She made friends everywhere she went.

She loved makeup and music. She loved helping people, talking with her friends and—perhaps most of all—making people laugh.

And that laugh.

Kylee had one of those ridiculous, contagious laughs that could fill a room. Sometimes you didn't even know what she was laughing about. It didn't matter. Before long, you would find yourself laughing right alongside her.

That was Kylee. She had a way of bringing light with her.

The Name That Stuck

Becoming Smilee Kylee

During the summer before her senior year, Kylee participated in a welding program. The people around her began calling her “Smilee Kylee” because of her smile and the energy she brought into the room.

The name stuck.

More importantly, Kylee discovered something she genuinely loved: welding. It gave her something to work toward and a future she could begin imagining for herself.

In 2025, Kylee graduated from Attleboro High School. She was incredibly proud of what she had accomplished.

She had also been chosen as Prom Queen—another glimpse of just how far that once quiet, shy little girl had come.

She was 18 years old. She had dreams. She had plans. Her adult life was only beginning.

Then she learned she was going to have a baby.

A New Future

The Baby Who Changed Her Future

Kylee had struggled with her mental health. During one particularly difficult period, she went to Butler Hospital after experiencing suicidal thoughts.

While she was there, Kylee learned that she was pregnant.

Her struggles did not simply disappear. But something in the way Kylee began looking toward the future changed.

From the way she spoke and acted afterward, her family saw the pregnancy give her a renewed sense of purpose and a future she desperately wanted to reach. There was someone else she was already beginning to love.

Kylee shared pictures of the hospital paperwork confirming her pregnancy with her family. She looked at baby clothes and talked about the baby constantly.

“If it’s a girl, I want to name her Eleanor and call her Ella.”

She even created a social-media post using something deeply personal to her—her beloved Elmo blanket. She shaped the blanket into a heart and placed the words “Baby Groom” in the middle.

Kylee was already imagining herself as a mother.

She planned to put welding on hold because she did not want to do anything she believed could put her baby at risk. The career she had become so excited about could wait. Her baby came first.

She should have gotten the chance to find out what kind of mother she would become.

She should have gotten to feel her baby grow. She should have gotten to prepare for the baby's arrival, buy tiny clothes, choose names, and call her family with every new milestone.

She should have gotten to hold her baby for the first time.

We can imagine that moment so clearly: Kylee smiling through happy tears while the people who loved her filled a hospital room, everyone waiting for their turn to hold the little person she had spent months waiting to meet.

She should have had that moment.

She should have had her welding career. She should have gotten married if that was what she wanted, grown older, made mistakes, changed her mind about things, celebrated birthdays, laughed until she cried, and created thousands of ordinary memories.

Kylee should have had a lifetime.

Instead, Kylee died at only 18 years old. Her unborn baby died with her.

The Things We Remember

The Little Things That Were Kylee

Some of the things we remember most aren't the big milestones. They're the little things.

Kylee had two things she couldn't sleep without: Bob, her beloved Minion stuffed animal, and her Elmo blanket.

That blanket wasn't just a blanket. Kylee's own parents had wrapped her in it when she was a baby. Years later, she still held onto it. When she learned she was pregnant, that little piece of her childhood took on another meaning.

Kylee wanted to wrap her own baby in it someday.

When she was 16, she once made her sister Faith drive all the way home to get Bob and the Elmo blanket because she couldn't sleep without them.

It's the kind of story that makes us smile because it was so completely Kylee.

Bob Her beloved Minion stuffed animal and one of the comforts she carried with her from childhood.
Her Elmo Blanket The blanket her parents wrapped her in as a baby—and the blanket she dreamed of someday wrapping around her own child.
Pieces of Home They weren't just objects. They connected Kylee to her childhood, her mom and dad, and the feeling of home.

Neither Bob nor the Elmo blanket was recovered after Kylee's death. Sometimes, it's the smallest things that hurt the most.

Because grief isn't only missing the person you lost. It's missing all the ordinary things that belonged to them—and all the moments that were supposed to come next.

Remember Her This Way

Kylee Was More Than How Her Life Ended

Kylee was more than a missing-person poster. More than a headline. More than a court case. And more than the terrible circumstances surrounding her death.

She was the little girl who laughed for years about her dad calling her “Fat Baby.”

She was a daughter who loved her parents and carried pieces of them with her long after they were gone.

She was a sister, a niece, a granddaughter, and a friend.

She loved makeup and music. She loved helping people and talking with her friends. She loved welding. She loved her cat, Bleb. She loved Bob and her Elmo blanket.

She was Prom Queen.

She was an expectant mother already dreaming about the child she hoped to meet.

And she was Smilee Kylee—the girl whose smile became so much a part of who she was that other people turned it into her name.

Kylee knew darkness. She experienced grief, instability, and struggles that many people never saw.

But darkness was never all that she was.

She could still laugh. She could still love. She could still dream about tomorrow.

Toward the end of her life, she was beginning to imagine a future that included a career, a baby, and a life of her own.

That future was taken from her. Her identity was not.

Her Legacy

Her Memory Becomes Someone Else's Hope.

We cannot give Kylee the lifetime she deserved.

We cannot go backward and change the moments when she needed more help, more understanding, more protection, or simply someone to recognize how much she was struggling.

But we can decide what we do with everything her life taught us.

That is where Smilee Kylee begins.

We want Kylee's name to become connected not only to the tragedy of how her life ended, but to what might still be possible for another woman whose story is still being written.

A woman struggling with her mental health. A woman who is pregnant and frightened about what comes next. A woman who needs somewhere safe to go. A woman trying to recover from trauma, instability, abuse, or addiction. A woman who has made mistakes or experienced setbacks. A woman who simply needs someone to keep showing up.

We cannot become the people Kylee needed soon enough to change her story.

But we can try to become those people for someone else.

That is why this project carries her nickname. That is why it carries her smile.

And that is why we want everyone who encounters her name to understand that there was an entire life behind it.

A funny, complicated, deeply loved young woman. A daughter. A sister. A friend. A welder with a future ahead of her. An expectant mother who never got the chance to hold her baby.

A girl named Kylee.

That is her legacy.
That is Smilee Kylee.

Her memory becomes someone else's hope.
